About

Hi, I'm Alex Thompson

I'm a software engineer in Denver, Colorado. I built this tool because I was obsessed with tracking my BMI — and then realized BMI alone was lying to me.

My Story

In 2023, I started tracking my BMI every single morning. Same time, same scale, same routine. For 365 days straight. The number said I was "normal." My doctor said I was "healthy."

But I had a belly. Love handles. And I was winded running up stairs.

Then I bought a $30 body fat caliper on Amazon. Measured my waist, neck, and chest. Plugged it into the Navy formula.

22% body fat. For a 28-year-old guy, that's borderline overweight. My BMI said "normal," but my body fat said "do something."

I had what doctors call "skinny fat." Normal BMI, high body fat. Way more common than people think.

What I Changed

I didn't go crazy. I kept my 5K runs but added two days of strength training in my living room while watching Netflix. Pushups, squats, dumbbell rows. Nothing fancy.

I also stopped eating like a rabbit. Eating 1200 calories of salad leaves you skinny fat — your body burns muscle instead of fat.

I upped my protein to about 150g per day. Chicken, eggs, Greek yogurt. Boring but effective.

Three months later: BMI went up to 24.1, but body fat dropped to 16%. I looked better, felt better, ran faster.

Why I Built This

I'm a software engineer. When something annoys me, I build a tool. So I made a BMI calculator that also estimates body fat percentage using the Navy method.

It saves your history in your browser. It charts your trends. It exports your data. And it's free.

No account needed. No data sent to any server. Your health data stays on your device.
