I Trained for a Half Marathon. My BMI Went Up. Here's Why.

Okay so this is embarrassing but I'm writing it anyway because maybe someone else is confused by the same thing.

I started training for the Denver Half in January. Running 4 days a week, cross-training, the whole thing. I was 74kg at 175cm, BMI 24.1, right at the top of "normal." My goal was to get to 70kg by race day. Classic "running makes you skinny" logic, right?

Race day was last Saturday. I finished in 2:08, which I'm genuinely proud of. First half marathon, didn't die, only walked once up a stupid hill at mile 10.

I weighed myself Monday morning. 76.2kg. BMI 24.9. Almost "overweight."

I stared at the scale for like five minutes. I ran 300+ miles over three months and GAINED two kilos. What the actual hell.

I almost threw my scale out the window. Then I remembered I have a body fat calculator in my app (Tab 2, Navy method, I built it myself because of course I did). Measured my waist: 82cm. Neck: 38cm. Plugged it in.

Body fat: 15.8%.

Three months ago? 18.4%.

So I gained 2.2kg of muscle, lost fat, and my BMI went from 24.1 to 24.9. The exact OPPOSITE of what I expected.

I spent the rest of Monday reading about this. Apparently it's super common for new runners to gain weight initially because muscle development outpaces fat loss in the first 8-12 weeks. Your legs, your core, even your arms from pumping. Plus glycogen storage increases, which binds water. I was probably carrying an extra 1-1.5kg of water weight just from training adaptation.

The frustrating part? If I only tracked BMI, I'd think I failed. I'd think running "doesn't work" for weight loss. I'd probably quit, like a lot of people do.

But I track body fat too. And that number told the real story. I'm leaner. I'm faster. I ran 13.1 miles without stopping. BMI is just... incomplete.

I'm not saying BMI is useless. For population-level health screening? Fine. For tracking my personal running transformation? Completely misleading.

My new rule: if my waist measurement goes down and my running pace goes down, I don't care what the scale says. Actually I do care, but I care about the right numbers now.

Next race is a 10K in July. Goal isn't weight-related anymore. Just beat my PR. And maybe don't panic when BMI fluctuates.

That's all for now.
