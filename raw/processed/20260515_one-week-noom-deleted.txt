I Tried Noom for 7 Days. Here's Why I Deleted It.

Noom's everywhere right now. Ads on every podcast, Instagram influencers pushing it, my mom even mentioned it. "Psychology-based weight loss," they say. "Change your relationship with food." Sounded interesting. I'm a data person, but I also know my willpower is garbage at 9pm when there's ice cream in the freezer.

I signed up for the free trial. $0 for 14 days, then $70 for 3 months. Fine, I'll set a calendar reminder to cancel.

Day 1: The onboarding quiz is... a lot. They ask about your eating habits, your goals, your "psychological triggers." I felt like I was in therapy but without the confidentiality guarantee. Where's this data going? Noom's privacy policy is 12 pages. I skimmed it. Shouldn't have skimmed it.

Day 2: The color system started. Green foods, yellow foods, red foods. Greek yogurt? Green. Almonds? RED. ALMONDS. Because they're calorie-dense. Never mind that they're literally good for you. I ate 15 almonds as a snack and the app treated it like I'd committed a crime. Meanwhile, diet soda is green because it's low calorie. The logic broke my brain.

Day 3: The daily lessons began. "Why do you eat when you're not hungry?" I don't know, Noom, maybe because food tastes good and existence is exhausting? The lessons take 10-15 minutes. I tried to do them during lunch. Felt like homework.

Day 4: Weigh-in day. The app asked me to log my weight, then immediately gave me a "trend analysis" based on... four days of data. Four. Days. The confidence interval on that trend is basically meaningless. As an engineer, this physically hurt me.

Day 5: The coach messaged me. "How are you feeling about your progress?" I hadn't made progress. It had been five days. I eat the same thing every day basically. I told the coach I was frustrated by the color system. They sent me a pre-written article about "food neutrality." Not helpful.

Day 6: I cheated. Ate pizza with friends. Logged it honestly. The app turned the entire day red and suggested I "make up for it" tomorrow. That's... not how metabolism works? One meal doesn't ruin anything. The guilt-trip coding in this app is impressive and toxic.

Day 7: I opened the app, looked at the red day from yesterday, looked at my calendar reminder to cancel, and just... deleted the whole thing. Didn't even finish the trial.

Here's the thing: Noom works for some people. My cousin lost 30lb with it. But for me? I'm already tracking everything. I already know my patterns. I don't need an app to tell me almonds are "red" when my body fat percentage says I'm doing fine.

What I need is data integrity. Consistent measurements. Long-term trends. Not color-coded shame.

My spreadsheet doesn't have a psychology degree. But it also doesn't charge $70 every three months to tell me I'm bad at eating.

Back to tracking.
