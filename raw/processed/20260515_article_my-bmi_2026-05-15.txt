Why I Stopped Trusting BMI Alone (And Started Measuring Body Fat)

I tracked my BMI for 365 days straight. Every morning, same time, same scale. The number said I was "normal." My doctor said I was "healthy." But I looked in the mirror and saw something different.

Here's what nobody tells you about BMI.

The Problem With My "Normal" BMI

At 5'10" and 165 lbs, my BMI was 23.7. Solidly in the normal range. But I had this belly that wouldn't go away. Love handles. And when I tried to run up the stairs to my apartment in Denver, I was winded.

I ran 5K three times a week. I ate salads for lunch. But something was off.

Then I bought a $30 body fat caliper on Amazon. Just curious, you know? I measured my abdomen, thigh, and chest. Plugged the numbers into a calculator.

22% body fat.

For a 28-year-old guy, that's borderline overweight. My BMI said "normal," but my body fat said "dude, you need to do something."

What BMI Doesn't See

BMI only cares about height and weight. It doesn't know if that weight is muscle or fat. It doesn't know where the fat is sitting. And it definitely doesn't know that visceral fat—the stuff around your organs—is way more dangerous than the fat under your skin.

I had what doctors call "skinny fat." Normal BMI, high body fat percentage. It's way more common than people think. Studies say up to 25% of normal-BMI adults are actually metabolically obese.

Yeah, I looked it up. I'm that guy now.

What I Changed

I didn't go crazy. I kept my 5K runs but added two days of strength training. Nothing fancy—pushups, squats, dumbbell rows in my living room while watching Netflix.

I also stopped eating like a rabbit. Turns out, eating 1200 calories of salad leaves you skinny fat. Your body burns muscle instead of fat. Who knew?

I upped my protein to about 150g per day. Chicken, eggs, Greek yogurt. Boring but effective.

Three months later, I measured again. BMI: 24.1 (slightly higher). Body fat: 16%. 

Wait, my BMI went UP? But I looked better. Felt better. Ran faster. The belly was shrinking.

That's when I stopped caring about BMI.

The Calculator I Built

Look, I'm a software engineer. When something annoys me, I build a tool. So I made a BMI calculator that also estimates body fat percentage. It uses the Navy method—waist, neck, height for guys, plus hip for women.

Is it perfect? No. DEXA scans are perfect, and they cost $150. My calculator is free and gets you within 3-4%. Good enough to track trends.

I use it every Sunday morning now. Same time, same conditions. The number matters less than the direction it's moving.

What You Should Do

If your BMI is "normal" but you feel sluggish, grab a tape measure. Measure your waist at belly button level. If it's over 40 inches for guys or 35 for women, that's a red flag regardless of BMI.

Or just look in the mirror. Be honest with yourself. BMI is a starting point, not the finish line.

I still track my BMI. Old habits. But I care more about the body fat number now. And honestly? I care most about how I feel running up those stairs.

No more winded. That's the real win.
