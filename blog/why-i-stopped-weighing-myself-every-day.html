Why I Stopped Weighing Myself Every Day

Okay so. I used to weigh myself every morning. Like, roll out of bed, shuffle to the bathroom, step on the scale before I even peed. Every. Single. Day.

The number ruled me.

178.2? Great day. I'd be productive, confident, maybe even go for a run. 180.1? Disaster. Irritable. Hated myself. Ate sad salad for lunch.

This went on for six months. The scale was my therapist, my judge, my executioner. Exhausting.

Then one Tuesday — I weighed myself at 6 AM: 179.4. Fine. Whatever. But I got curious at 8 PM after dinner: 183.7. Same day. Four pounds. I freaked out. Ate nothing but celery for 24 hours. Felt terrible.

That's when it hit me: daily weighing is noise, not signal.

Your weight bounces 2-5 pounds daily. Water. Salt. Whether you pooped. Sleep. Stress. It's like checking your stock portfolio every minute — you'll go insane.

So I switched to weekly. Saturday mornings. Same conditions. After bathroom, before breakfast, same clothes (or naked, honestly). The trend became clear. Noise gone.

My log:

  • Week 1: 180.2
  • Week 2: 179.8
  • Week 3: 179.1
  • Week 4: 178.5

Down 1.7 pounds. Not dramatic. Not Instagram-worthy. But real.

Mental health improved more than physical. I stopped obsessing. Stopped letting a number ruin my day. Started focusing on how I felt, whether my clothes fit, if I could run stairs without dying.

If daily weighing makes you miserable, try weekly. Or monthly. Trend matters. Snapshot doesn't.

— Alex P.S. Scale's in the closet now. I pull it out Saturdays, use it, put it back. Out of sight, out of mind. Works great.

— Alex

Software Engineer, Denver. Just sharing what works for me.

Note: This is a personal diary, not medical advice. I'm not a doctor — I'm a guy who writes code and occasionally remembers to go to the gym. Talk to a real healthcare provider for actual health decisions.
