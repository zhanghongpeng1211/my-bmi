I Tracked My BMI for 90 Days Straight. Here's What Actually Happened.

I Tracked My BMI for 90 Days Straight. Here's What Actually Happened. 2026-05-19 BMI Tracking



Okay so full disclosure: I started this because I was bored at 2 AM on a Tuesday. Not exactly the most inspiring origin story, but hey, at least I'm honest about it.

I had just finished debugging some garbage legacy code (you know the type — 4000 lines of spaghetti, zero comments, someone named "Dave" wrote it in 2019 and apparently vanished off the face of the earth). My eyes were burning. I was eating my third energy bar of the night. And I stepped on the scale out of pure self-loathing.

BMI: 24.3. Not terrible, but not great either. Right on the edge of "overweight" for my height.

So I did what any rational sleep-deprived software engineer would do: I opened Excel. Or well, Google Sheets. Same thing.



I didn't just want to track BMI. I wanted . Trends. Predictive modeling. I literally built a dashboard with conditional formatting that turns cells red when my BMI goes above 24. My girlfriend walked by and asked if I was working. I said yes. Technically it was personal project work.

Here's what I actually tracked:

  • Morning weight (after bathroom, before coffee — consistency matters)
  • Height (spoiler: didn't change)
  • BMI (auto-calculated because I'm not doing math at 7 AM)
  • Sleep hours (from my watch that I now only use for sleep tracking)
  • Workout type (if any — "none" appeared way more than I'd like to admit)
  • Mood score (1-10, totally subjective, usually correlated with whether deploy went smooth)



 BMI hovered between 24.1 and 24.4. I was "trying" to eat better but also ordering DoorDash at 11 PM because "I deserved it after that sprint." My mood scores averaged 6.2. My code quality probably averaged lower.

 Something clicked around day 34. I started meal prepping on Sundays. Not fancy stuff — literally chicken, rice, broccoli in containers. But the consistency helped. BMI started a slow downward trend: 24.0... 23.8... 23.6. I got weirdly excited seeing the numbers drop. Like, I'd update the sheet and screenshot it. My girlfriend thought I was insane. She wasn't wrong.

 Hit 23.0 on day 67. Felt like I'd unlocked an achievement. Kept the meal prep going, added 20-minute walks during lunch (working from home in Denver means I can actually see mountains while walking, so that's nice). By day 90: . Solidly in the "normal" range.



1.  On weeks where I averaged <6 hours, my weight stalled or went up. Even if I was eating the same food. Cortisol? Probably. I'm not a doctor, I'm just a guy who stares at spreadsheets.

2.  I had two weekends where I just... didn't track. Ate whatever. Both times I expected to see a spike. Nope. BMI barely moved. But when I "cheated" by stress-eating chips during a production outage? That showed up. The context of the eating mattered more than the calories.

3.  It was the habit of . The 30 seconds every morning created awareness. I'd think twice about that second beer because I knew I'd see the number tomorrow. Not in a disordered way — in a "I'm collecting data about myself" way. Which is basically what engineers do with everything.



I missed 7 days total. Day 14 because I was sick. Day 38 because I was on a camping trip and forgot the scale. Day 52, 53, 54 because I was in a depressive slump after a project got cancelled and I couldn't bring myself to care. Day 71 because I straight up forgot. Day 83 because I was traveling for a conference.

The gaps are in the spreadsheet. I didn't delete them. They tell a story too.



I'm not "done." BMI 22.1 is fine but I want to see if I can maintain this without the obsessive tracking. So I'm switching to weekly weigh-ins instead of daily. Still using the [BMI calculator](https://my-bmi.net) when I need quick math (because who can divide by height squared before coffee?), but the daily spreadsheet is retired.

Actually, I might build a simple web app to replace the spreadsheet. Something that charts the trend automatically. If I do, I'll post it here. Or maybe I won't. Depends on whether I get distracted by another side project.



  • Start: 5'10", 169 lbs, BMI 24.3
  • End: 5'10", 154 lbs, BMI 22.1
  • Total change: -15 lbs over 90 days
  • Average rate: ~1.2 lbs/week (sustainable, not crash diet territory)
  • Workouts per week: Started at 0, ended at 3 (mix of walking and dumbbell stuff in my garage)
  • Energy bars consumed: Significantly fewer. Replaced with actual meals. Revolutionary concept.

---

If you read this far, you're probably also someone who overthinks health stuff. Welcome to the club. It's exhausting but weirdly satisfying.

I'll probably write about the meal prep system next. Or maybe about why I stopped using calorie tracking apps (spoiler: they made me miserable). Follow along if you want — no pressure, I'm not trying to be an influencer, I just type a lot when I'm procrastinating on actual work.

P.S. If anyone from my team reads this: yes, I built the dashboard during work hours. It was a "mental health break." Don't @ me.

P.P.S. The legacy code is still garbage. Some things never change.

— Alex Software Engineer, Denver BMI tracker, spreadsheet enthusiast, professional overthinker
