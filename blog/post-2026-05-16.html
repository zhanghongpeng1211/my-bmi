Why I Ditched My Smartwatch Calorie Tracking (And What I Use Instead)

Okay so I need to rant about something. For like 18 months I was OBSESSED with my Garmin's calorie estimates. Every run, every walk, every time I stood up to get coffee — I was checking how many calories I burned. And you know what? It made me fatter. Well not fatter exactly but it definitely messed with my head.

Here's the thing. My watch said I burned 3200 calories on days I ran 5 miles. So I'd eat 2700 and feel good about my 500 deficit. But my weight didn't budge. For MONTHS. My BMI stayed at 27.4 like it was glued there. I was so frustrated I almost threw the watch into Sloan's Lake.

The Problem With Calorie Estimates

Turns out — and this took me way too long to figure out — fitness trackers overestimate calorie burn by like 20-40%. There's actual studies on this. I think I read one from Stanford? Or maybe it was UCSF? Anyway the point is they're wrong. A lot.

My watch thought my basal metabolic rate was 2100 calories. My actual BMR calculated from my body composition scan? 1840. That's a 260 calorie difference EVERY DAY. No wonder I wasn't losing weight. I was eating at maintenance thinking I was in a deficit.

What I Do Now

I stopped tracking exercise calories completely. Like zero. I only track my food intake using a simple spreadsheet (yeah I'm a software engineer, I made a spreadsheet, judge me). I aim for 1800 calories daily regardless of exercise. No "earning" food through workouts. No 10,000 step celebrations with extra snacks.

And here's the wild part — my BMI started moving. Dropped from 27.4 to 26.1 in about 10 weeks. Same exercise routine. Same foods mostly. Just stopped trusting that lying piece of wrist technology.

My New Rules

Track food, not exercise calories

Use a weekly weight average, not daily (my scale jumps around like crazy)

Focus on consistency, not perfection

Walk 8k steps daily, no calorie "credit" for it

I'm not saying smartwatches are useless. My heart rate data is actually helpful. Sleep tracking is... okay I guess? But the calorie stuff? Nope. Done. Never again.

Anyway if you're stuck like I was, maybe try ignoring your watch's calorie numbers for a month. See what happens. Worst case you save some battery life.

Oh and follow along if you want — I post my weekly weigh-ins and random thoughts here. No schedule, just when something interesting happens.

— Alex, software engineer in Denver, currently BMI 26.1 and way less stressed about numbers
